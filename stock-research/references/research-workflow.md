# Stock Research Workflow

Use this reference for deeper stock research, stock comparisons, earnings work, watchlists, and investment memos.

## 1. Define the Job

Clarify the task before collecting data:

- Quick view: answer whether the setup is attractive and what to watch.
- Deep dive: build a fundamental thesis with valuation and risks.
- Earnings preview/review: focus on expectations, reported results, guidance, and revisions.
- Technical setup: focus on trend, levels, volume, volatility, and trade invalidation.
- Comparison: normalize metrics across peers and explain why one is stronger or weaker.
- Watchlist: rank names by thesis quality, catalyst timing, valuation, liquidity, and risk.

Always specify the market and ticker format when ambiguous. Examples: `AAPL` for US, `0700.HK` for Hong Kong, `600519.SH` or `000001.SZ` for A-shares.

## 2. Source Routing

Prefer primary and official sources for current facts:

- US stocks: SEC EDGAR filings, company investor-relations pages, earnings releases, Nasdaq/NYSE pages.
- A-shares: CNINFO, Shanghai/Shenzhen/Beijing Stock Exchange disclosures, company announcements, official financial reports.
- Hong Kong stocks: HKEXnews, company investor-relations pages, exchange announcements.
- Macro and rates: official central banks, statistics bureaus, treasury/ministry sources, FRED when relevant.
- Market data: reputable quote providers; cross-check unusual moves, splits, dividends, and after-hours data.

Use news and analyst commentary as secondary context. Label them as market interpretation rather than primary facts.

## 3. Factual Base

Collect only the facts needed for the user's task:

- Price, daily move, 52-week range, market cap, volume, liquidity, and major corporate actions.
- Business segments, revenue mix, geography, and key drivers.
- Revenue growth, gross/operating margin, EPS, free cash flow, net cash/debt, dilution, buybacks, and dividends.
- Latest quarterly/annual results and guidance.
- Consensus expectations when available, but separate consensus from company-reported facts.
- Recent news, management comments, regulatory events, lawsuits, product launches, and industry data.

Watch for stale numbers. If the latest filing or earnings release postdates a data provider snapshot, use the filing/release as authoritative.

## 4. Thesis Construction

Build a balanced thesis:

- One-sentence thesis: what must be true for the stock to work.
- Bull case: upside drivers and why the market may underappreciate them.
- Bear case: downside drivers and why the market may be right or too optimistic.
- Variant perception: what differs from consensus or price action.
- Time horizon: when the thesis should start proving or failing.
- Invalidation: facts or levels that would make the thesis wrong.

Avoid vague claims such as "good company" or "cheap valuation" without the metric, peer, historical range, and reason.

## 5. Valuation

Choose valuation methods that fit the business:

- Profitable mature companies: P/E, EV/EBIT, free-cash-flow yield, dividend yield, buyback yield.
- Growth companies: EV/Sales, gross profit multiple, Rule of 40, margin path, long-term free-cash-flow potential.
- Banks/insurers: P/B, ROE, asset quality, capital adequacy, net interest margin, reserve coverage.
- Cyclicals: mid-cycle earnings, replacement cost, commodity sensitivity, balance sheet durability.
- REITs/infrastructure: FFO/AFFO, cap rate, occupancy, debt maturity, interest-rate sensitivity.

Compare against peers, history, and growth/quality. Do not call a stock cheap only because a multiple is below one peer.

## 6. Technical and Flow Checks

Use technicals as risk management and timing context:

- Trend: above/below major moving averages; higher highs/lows or lower highs/lows.
- Levels: support, resistance, breakout, failed breakout, gaps, and volume-by-price areas.
- Momentum: RSI/MACD or simpler price momentum, but do not overfit indicators.
- Volume/liquidity: confirm whether a move has participation.
- Volatility: ATR, gap risk around events, options-implied move when options are in scope.

Tie technical observations to actionability: entry zone, invalidation level, partial profit zone, or "no trade until confirmation."

## 7. Catalysts and Calendar

List time-bound events:

- Earnings date, guidance updates, investor day, product launch, regulatory decision, index inclusion/exclusion.
- Macro data, central-bank meetings, commodity price moves, FX changes, policy announcements.
- Lockup expiration, financing, share repurchase window, dividend record/ex-date.

When dates are uncertain, state uncertainty and verify before finalizing a trade plan.

## 8. Final Synthesis

End with a decision framework:

- Rating style: constructive / neutral / cautious, or setup quality from 1-5.
- Confidence: low / medium / high, with why.
- Scenarios: upside/base/downside with drivers and rough valuation implications if data supports it.
- What to monitor: 3-6 metrics, events, or levels.
- What changes the mind: explicit invalidation conditions.

If the user asks "买不买" or "能不能上车", answer with conditions and risk budget, not a command.
